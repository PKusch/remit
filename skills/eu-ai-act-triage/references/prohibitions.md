# Article 5 — prohibited practices

Applicable since **2 February 2025**. Penalties reach **€35,000,000 or 7% of total
worldwide annual turnover**, whichever is higher (Art. 99) — the heaviest tier in the
Regulation.

> **Two further prohibitions apply from 2 December 2026**, added by the Digital Omnibus
> on AI (in force 27 July 2026): AI systems that generate or manipulate realistic
> material depicting an identifiable person's intimate parts without their explicit
> consent, and systems generating child sexual abuse material. These are listed as
> (i) and (j) below and are commonly absent from guidance written before August 2026.

Check these before any other classification step. A prohibited practice is not a severe
form of high-risk; it is a system that may not be placed on the market, put into
service, or used at all.

---

## The eight prohibitions

**(a) Subliminal, manipulative, or deceptive techniques** that materially distort
behaviour by appreciably impairing the ability to make an informed decision, causing a
person to take a decision they would not otherwise have taken, in a manner that causes
or is reasonably likely to cause significant harm.

**(b) Exploiting vulnerabilities** due to age, disability, or a specific social or
economic situation, with the same materially-distorting effect and significant-harm
threshold.

**(c) Social scoring** — evaluating or classifying people or groups over time based on
social behaviour or personal characteristics, where the resulting score leads to
detrimental treatment either (i) in social contexts unrelated to where the data was
generated, or (ii) that is unjustified or disproportionate to the behaviour.

**(d) Predicting criminal offending risk based solely on profiling** a person or
assessing their personality traits. Does not apply where the system supports a human
assessment already grounded in objective, verifiable facts directly linked to a criminal
activity.

**(e) Untargeted scraping of facial images** from the internet or CCTV to build or expand
facial recognition databases.

**(f) Emotion inference in the workplace and in education institutions**, except where
intended for medical or safety reasons.

**(g) Biometric categorisation** that categorises people individually on the basis of
their biometric data to deduce or infer race, political opinions, trade union membership,
religious or philosophical beliefs, sex life, or sexual orientation. Does not cover
labelling or filtering of lawfully acquired biometric datasets, or categorisation by law
enforcement.

**(h) Real-time remote biometric identification in publicly accessible spaces for law
enforcement**, subject to narrow exceptions (targeted search for specific victims,
prevention of specific and imminent threat to life or a terrorist attack, and locating
or identifying suspects of listed serious offences) — and those exceptions require prior
judicial or independent administrative authorisation, a fundamental rights impact
assessment, and registration.

**(i) Non-consensual intimate imagery** — generating or manipulating realistic images,
video, audio, or similar material depicting an identifiable person's intimate parts,
without that person's explicit consent. *Applies from 2 December 2026.*

**(j) Child sexual abuse material** — systems generating CSAM. *Applies from
2 December 2026.*

---

## How to use this

**The commercial ones catch people out.** (a), (b), (c), and (f) are not exotic
law-enforcement scenarios. Dark-pattern optimisation targeted at people in financial
distress, engagement systems that materially distort behaviour to a person's significant
detriment, cross-context behavioural scoring that affects service access, and sentiment
analysis of employees on calls or in the office — all sit close to these lines and all
are things ordinary companies build.

**The thresholds matter and are load-bearing.** (a) and (b) both require material
distortion *and* significant harm, actual or reasonably likely. Many aggressive systems
will not meet that bar. Do not flatten the test to "this feels manipulative" — say which
limb is engaged and on what facts.

**Where a system is plausibly close to any of these, stop and escalate to legal.** Do not
continue into high-risk assessment as if the question were one of degree, and do not
resolve it yourself. The right output is a clearly stated concern, the sub-paragraph
engaged, the facts relied on, and a recommendation that qualified counsel take it.

**Recital context matters here more than elsewhere.** These prohibitions carry extensive
recitals narrowing and explaining their scope, and Commission guidelines on prohibited
practices have been issued. Work from the current guidance rather than from the bare
text.
