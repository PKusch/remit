# DORA — article map

Working reference at chapter/article level, in the same spirit as the EU AI Act
obligations file: enough structure to point an assessment at the right article,
not a substitute for the regulation. **Consult Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 itself
for the operative text**, and the related Regulatory Technical Standards (RTS)
and Implementing Technical Standards (ITS), which carry the operational detail
these article numbers only gesture at.

DORA applies from 17 January 2025. Unlike the EU AI Act, its dates were not
touched by the Digital Omnibus — it is a separate regulation on a separate
track.

## Chapter map

| Chapter | Articles | Subject |
|---|---|---|
| I | 1–4 | Scope, definitions, proportionality |
| II | 5–16 | ICT risk management |
| III | 17–23 | ICT-related incident management, classification and reporting |
| IV | 24–27 | Digital operational resilience testing |
| V | 28–44 | Managing ICT third-party risk (contracts, and oversight of critical providers) |
| VI | 45 | Information-sharing arrangements |
| VII | 46–56 | Competent authorities |

## Chapter II — ICT risk management (Art. 5–16)

| Article | Concern |
|---|---|
| 5 | Governance and organisation — the management body owns and is accountable for the ICT risk management framework, not just the CISO function |
| 6 | The ICT risk management framework itself — documented, reviewed at least yearly, audited |
| 8 | Identification — mapping ICT assets, dependencies, and the business functions they support |
| 9 | Protection and prevention |
| 10 | Detection — mechanisms to spot anomalous activity promptly |
| 11 | Response and recovery, including a business continuity policy |
| 12 | Backup policies, restoration and recovery procedures |
| 13 | Learning and evolving, **including Art. 13(6): ICT security awareness programmes and digital operational resilience training for all staff and management, proportionate to role** |
| 14 | Communication — crisis communication plans for a major incident |
| 16 | A simplified framework for a defined set of smaller entities |

## Chapter III — incident management (Art. 17–23)

| Article | Concern |
|---|---|
| 17 | The ICT-related incident management process itself |
| 18 | Classification of incidents and cyber threats against common criteria |
| 19 | Reporting major ICT-related incidents to the competent authority |
| 20 | Harmonisation of reporting content and templates (via RTS/ITS) |
| 21 | Centralisation of reporting through a single EU hub (in development) |
| 22 | Supervisory feedback on an entity's incident handling |
| 23 | Voluntary reporting of significant cyber threats |

## Chapter IV — resilience testing (Art. 24–27)

| Article | Concern |
|---|---|
| 24 | General testing requirements — proportionate to size, risk, scale |
| 25 | Testing of ICT tools and systems: vulnerability assessments, scenario-based tests, source-code reviews where feasible |
| 26 | Advanced testing by threat-led penetration testing (TLPT) — for entities identified as significant |
| 27 | Requirements for the testers who perform TLPT |

## Chapter V — ICT third-party risk (Art. 28–44)

| Article | Concern |
|---|---|
| 28 | General principles — the entity stays fully responsible for compliance even when it outsources the ICT function itself |
| 29 | Preliminary assessment of concentration risk before signing |
| 30 | Key contractual provisions — audit rights, exit strategies, service levels, sub-outsourcing chains, and (for critical or important functions) more specific terms |
| 31–44 | Oversight framework for **critical** ICT third-party providers — a Lead Overseer regime run by the European Supervisory Authorities, not something an individual firm administers |

## What this file does not cover

Chapters VI and VII are competent-authority and cross-border machinery, not
obligations an assessed firm carries directly, so they are named in the
chapter map above and not broken out further here.
